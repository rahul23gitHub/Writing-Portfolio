Artist statement

Why I write

My writing is about rhythms. Our heartbeats follow a rhythm. Our body has a circadian rhythm. The earth revolving and rotating has a rhythm. And I try to bring out this rhythm to my work through poetry and songs. The stories I write are a mix of my personal experiences and that of the world’s history. The world too has a rhythm, and what goes away as history is never truly lost. It comes back and influences us in ways we do not know. I write to bring into light the fact that maybe the way of life has changed, but our feelings and thoughts, which define us as humans, have not.

I am currently in my final year dual degree student at IIT Bombay in the Electrical Engineering department. I have participated in many institute competitions and have written songs and poems for plays and bands, and at all times my motive was a break-away from the constant stress I experienced from academic pressure I did not consciously opt for. I have written and performed poems throughout my school life, and my poems represented an innocence which a child has. My poems were never philosophical or well thought out, because life back then did not need me to. Whether or not the poem was metaphorical, it always had rhyming. That innocence was lost when I opted for the JEE race. And my participation in these plays was a way for me to remind myself of a version that still exists down within myself.

My songs and poems have rhyming because without rhyming it always feels like a spot inside me is empty and it has not received the closure that it deserved. I also never take the easy way of writing a line with more words than it should just for the sake of exposure. I believe that each syllable in a sentence deserves the right amount of time, and I write to preserve the rhythm of the song that way.

Writing stories and scripts is something I am comparatively new to. I actively read books, articles, and newspapers to immerse myself into the art of storytelling through text passages. My storytelling journey began in my childhood when me and my friends used to make angry bird comic books with papers torn out of our school notebooks. Those comic books were the purest form of imagination my childhood version had. With time, my art segregated into words and drawings, with the words taking forms of poetry and songs, while art somehow remained a doodling pass time rather than something I became highly skilled at. With words, I was able to take my art to people who were rather lazy to look at an art and interpret its meaning. With words, I was able to convey more easily than through a painting, mostly because with paintings I am too worried about it not looking good. But words have a fixed set of alphabets.

I see the universe as a playground. I believe that human beings are given a gift of life and consciousness by this universe, and we should at all points try to work towards making this place a happy and comfortable place for ourselves and others. Although it is a gift, no one came in this world with their own will. And therefore I believe that everyone should be allowed to believe what they believe and unless they cause any harm to anyone, they have the right to hold and practice their beliefs. Anyone does not hold the right to tell someone that according to their beliefs, they cannot do something. A child is born without any knowledge or wit of her own, and everyone around her starts infusing her mind with the beliefs they have, and this feeling of fear of failure to follow the beliefs is instilled at such a young age, that the child even when she has matured otherwise does not dare to question the beliefs that she was spoon fed in her childhood.

Art, of any form or age has done the task of asking those questions for her. Only when I began reading, or understanding paintings, or deciphering song lyrics, I began questioning the obvious questions that many people still fear. And although no one has answers, everyone seems to preach it. Thus, my poems, songs and stories allow me to express my own beliefs, and ask the questions that I always had, but never dared to ask. It is my way of opening the dam of questions in my mind and letting them flow into the dried up lake of society we live in.

My inspirations have been Javed Akhtar, Gulzar, Varun Grover, Khaled Houssini, Stan Lee, Michael Straczynski, Aamir Khan, Christopher Nolan, Denis Villeneuve, Monkey Magic, Veritasium. My childhood was made awesome by stories and poems by Mahadevi Varma, Premchand, Ruskin Bond, Rudyard Kipling, Kabirdas, and many more whose stories I still remember but not the names.

I want to work more on writing stories and screenplays. I make stories in my mind, but many times fail to write my ideas down. I also believe that with more writing and implementing those writings through short assignments and films, I am bound to improve my techniques and become a better writer each day.
